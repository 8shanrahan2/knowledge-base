# Clips and Arpeggiator

Serum 2 includes internal musical-control modules: **Clips** and **Arpeggiator**. These features turn Serum from a pure sound generator into a patch-contained performance and sequencing environment.

## Clips

The CLIP module is a piano-roll-like system for creating, managing, triggering, and recording note clips. The manual covers global parameters, creating new clip banks, saving clip banks, piano roll editing, grid size, zooming, folding, clip length, clip settings, clip management, triggering, recording, MIDI out, routing MIDI to another instrument, and clip-module configuration.

Use clips when:

- A patch should include its own phrase or rhythmic note behavior.
- You want repeatable internal MIDI material.
- You want to record or trigger patterns from within Serum.
- You want to route MIDI generated by Serum to another instrument.

## Arpeggiator

The arpeggiator creates pattern-based note motion from held notes or incoming MIDI. The manual covers arp banks, saving banks, setting arp patterns, creating custom patterns, loading/renaming/saving patterns, graph editing, transpose settings, playback settings, retrigger settings, velocity settings, arpeggiator management, triggering, MIDI out, and configuration.

Use the arpeggiator when:

- Held chords should become rhythmic sequences.
- A patch needs built-in pulse or melodic motion.
- You want performance control without drawing every note in the DAW piano roll.
- You want MIDI output from Serum to drive another instrument.

## How clips and arp differ

| Need | Prefer |
|---|---|
| Fixed piano-roll phrase | Clips |
| Chord-to-pattern behavior | Arpeggiator |
| Clip banks and recorded note material | Clips |
| Pattern variation from held notes | Arpeggiator |
| MIDI routing to another instrument | Either, depending on source |

## Sound-design implications

Clips and arpeggiators do more than trigger notes. They also interact with envelopes, LFO retrigger behavior, velocity modulation, note tracking, and MIDI output. A simple patch can sound much more complex when the note engine creates controlled movement.
